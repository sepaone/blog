<h2>What is SEPA?</h2>
<p>SEPA stands for Single Euro Payments Area, and is an initiative supported by the European Payments Council (EPC). The main purpose of this initiative is to <span style="color:#61acb1;"> <em>improve the efficiency of cross-border payments and turn the fragmented national markets for euro payments into a single domestic one</em> </span>. SEPA will enable customers to make cashless euro payments to anyone located anywhere in the area, using a single bank account and a single set of payment instruments. Economically speaking, this is very important in achieving economies of scale, by creating a common framework of payment instruments, standards and procedures.</p>
<hr />
<h2>Who is covered?</h2>
<p>The initiative covers a total of <span style="color:#61acb1;"> 34 member states</span>, which covers a population of 500+ million people. The following countries are covered:</p>
<blockquote>
<ul>
<li>The 18 states that are in the Eurozone</li>
<li>The 10 states not in the Eurozone (Bulgaria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Denmark, Hungary, Lithuania, Poland, Romania, Sweden, United Kingdom).</li>
<li>The 4 European Free Trade Association member states (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland)</li>
<li>Monaco, San Marino</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
<hr />
<h2>What is SEPA Direct Debit?</h2>
<p>SEPA Direct Debit is a pull based system. Once a customer signs a mandate, the merchant can initiate payments. These payments are bank to bank, ie, there are no card companies involved. Some key features of this system are:</p>
<blockquote>
<ul>
<li>All payments are in Euros</li>
<li>Customer protection; customers can demand a refund for unauthorized transactions for up to <span style="color:#61acb1;">13 months</span></li>
<li>IBAN/BIC is used rather than Account number and Bank codes.</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
<hr />
<h2>What are the different schemes in SEPA Direct Debit?</h2>
<p>There are 2 variants of the SEPA Direct Debit:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>SEPA Core Direct Debit (SDD CORE, SDD COR1)</strong></li>
<li><strong>SEPA B2B Direct Debit</strong></li>
</ol>
<p>The <span style="color:#61acb1;">SEPA Core Direct Debit </span>scheme is based on the following concept: “I request money from someone, with their prior approval, and credit it to myself”. The payer and the biller must both hold an account with a PSP within the SEPA Zone.</p>
<blockquote>
<ul>
<li>For the SDD CORE scheme, 1st time direct debits have to be submitted to the paying agent <span style="color:#61acb1;"> 5 business days </span> before the due date. For subsequent payments, this becomes <span style="color:#61acb1;"> 2 business days </span>. For one-time payments, the lead time is <span style="color:#61acb1;"> 5 business days</span>.</li>
<li>For the SDD COR1 scheme, the lead time is <span style="color:#61acb1;"> 1 business day</span> for all payments.</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
<p>The <span style="color:#61acb1;"> SEPA Direct Debit B2B </span>scheme allows business customers to make payments in the form of direct debit. It offers significant efficiency gains and automation of payment processing. This goes a great way in optimizing the cash management processes.</p>
<blockquote>
<ul>
<li>For the SDD B2B scheme, one-off, first time or subsequent direct debits must be submitted to the paying agent <span style="color:#61acb1;">1 business day </span> before the due date.</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
<p>The main differences between the 2 schemes are:</p>
<blockquote>
<ul>
<li>The B2B scheme is available only to businesses</li>
<li>In the B2B scheme, the payer is not entitled to a refund of an authorized transaction</li>
<li>The B2B scheme offers a significantly shorter processing time, tailored to the needs of the business community.</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
